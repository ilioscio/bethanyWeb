Our History

An Ontario faith community since 1904.

More Than a Century of Faith

✛

Bethany Presbyterian Church was organized in 1904, when Ontario was still a young railroad town on the edge of the Oregon desert.

Scroll down to walk through the moments that shaped us.

1904 The beginning

Bethany Presbyterian Church is organized

A Presbyterian congregation is formally organized in Ontario, Oregon, and the Rev. George W. Morrison is called as its first pastor.

War and Post-War Growth

1941 – 1945 Awaiting detail

The war years

Members who served, the congregation's wartime ministry, and Ontario's part in the story of the Treasure Valley during the Second World War.

1947 – 1958 Long pastorate

Eleven years with the Rev. Raymond L. Wilson

The longest pastorate to that point. The post-war years bring new families to Ontario, and with them a growing Sunday School and a congregation outgrowing its space.

1960 – 1989

A Generation of Growth

1963 – 1972

Nine years with the Rev. Wesley G. Jones

A decade of change in the wider church and the wider country — and a settled, growing congregation in Ontario.

1978 – 1999 Longest pastorate

Twenty-one years with the Rev. Thomas E. Young

The longest pastorate in Bethany's history. More than two decades of weddings, baptisms, confirmations, and funerals — a generation of this congregation grew up knowing one pastor's voice from the pulpit.

The Rev. Karen Francis

2016 – Today

Where We Are Now

2017

The Rev. Linda Toth is called to Bethany

Rev. Toth serves the congregation for nine years, including the long season of the COVID-19 pandemic, when worship moved online and the church found new ways to stay a community.

2026 This year

A season of transition

The Rev. Linda Toth preaches her last Sunday at Bethany on May 31, 2026, and retires. The congregation begins the search for a part-time pastor to lead its next chapter.

The next chapter

Bethany gathers each Sunday on the same corner it has occupied for generations — still praying, still singing, still setting out coffee afterward. The history isn't finished.
